# Personas

Six people: two customers whose needs pull in different directions (a big weekly shop,
and someone who depends on substitutions going right), and one for each side behind the
scenes. Each ends with the stories the persona drives.

## Priya, 38 — customer, weekly family shop

> "I book Thursday evening every week. If that slot's gone, my week falls apart."

Two kids, both parents working. One big shop a week, about 60 items, plus the odd
top-up. Builds the basket over a couple of days on her phone.

- **Goals:** her usual slot, every week; amend the order until the night before; a total
  she can predict.
- **Frustrations:** her slot gone while she was still shopping; six substitutes she didn't
  want; a charge higher than the checkout total.
- **Drives:** slot booking and reservation, amend before cutoff, substitution rules,
  held-then-charged payment.

## George, 74 — customer, lives alone, doesn't drive

> "If they're out of my semi-skimmed, don't send me oat milk."

Retired, shops online since his knee operation. Small, regular baskets, the same brands.
Reads the substitution email carefully.

- **Goals:** exactly what he ordered, or nothing; hear about changes before the van
  arrives; a driver who carries the bags in.
- **Frustrations:** surprise substitutes; short-dated milk; a minimum basket he struggles
  to reach.
- **Drives:** per-item "no substitute", substitution notice before arrival, accept or
  reject at the door, short-dated refunds.

## Grace, 45 — store operations, online operations lead

> "Every slot I sell is a promise my pickers and vans have to keep."

Runs the online side of a large store: the range online, how many slots to open, and
staffing pickers and vans to match.

- **Goals:** slots that fill without overbooking; peak slots priced to spread demand;
  fewer substitutions week on week.
- **Frustrations:** selling more slots than vans can run; lapsed reservations blocking
  real customers; the range online out of step with the shelf.
- **Drives:** range and prices, slot capacity and pricing, delivery area.

## Marek, 31 — picker, early shift

> "Give me the list in aisle order and tell me what they'll accept if it's gone."

Picks for three slots a morning with a trolley of totes and a handheld scanner. Paid by
the shift, measured by items per hour and accuracy.

- **Goals:** one walk through the store per slot; clear substitution rules per item;
  weighing without retyping prices.
- **Frustrations:** guessing what a customer would accept; zig-zagging the store; chilled
  totes left out too long.
- **Drives:** pick list, mark picked, weigh, substitute or mark missing, pack by
  temperature.

## Sam, 46 — van driver

> "Eight drops, one hour each. Lose ten minutes at the first door and I'm late for the rest."

Runs two routes a day from the store. Loads his own van, settles substitutions at the
door and checks ID.

- **Goals:** totes loaded in drop order; customers who know about substitutes before he
  knocks; a quick way to log a rejected item.
- **Frustrations:** doorstep debates about substitutes; nobody home with frozen in the
  van; being blamed for picking mistakes.
- **Drives:** load in drop order, run the route, hand over, rejects and ID checks,
  running late.

## Lin, 27 — support, customer-care agent

> "Show me what was ordered, picked, substituted and handed back. Then I can be fair."

Handles chats and calls about missing, damaged and short-dated items, and missed slots,
within refund rules the business sets.

- **Goals:** one timeline per order: ordered, picked, delivered, charged; refund an item
  in one action; see repeat issues by store or picker.
- **Frustrations:** not knowing whether an item was missing or rejected; refunds that
  don't match the charge.
- **Drives:** order lookup and timeline, item refunds, missed-slot compensation.
