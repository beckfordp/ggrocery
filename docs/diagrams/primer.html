gGrocery: a grocery-delivery primer and story map

A weekly shop, delivered in a one-hour slot. It looks like gshop's e-commerce until two things bite: delivery is booked against scarce time slots, and the shelf decides what the customer actually gets. What follows is the domain in one sitting, the people it serves, and a first cut of the map.

Part 1

The domain in one sitting

A grocery order is a promise made days ahead and kept in a single hour: the right food, fresh and cold where it should be, at the door inside the slot. Between the promise and the door, the shelf gets a vote. Items run out, chicken weighs what it weighs, and the customer's rules decide what stands in for what's missing.

vs gshop · 1

Slots, not just stock

The customer books a delivery hour before they shop. Each slot has a capacity; a reservation holds a place in it while the basket fills, then lapses if they don't check out.

vs gshop · 2

Substitution rules

Every line in the basket carries a rule for when it's out of stock: no substitute, anything similar, or a chosen alternative. The picker follows it; the customer accepts or rejects at the door.

vs gshop · 3

The price settles late

Weighed items and substitutions mean the total isn't known at checkout. The card is held for an estimate and charged for what was actually delivered.

Customer

Wants the whole week's shop in one delivery, at a time they're home, with nothing important missing. Judges the service by what's substituted and how.

Store operations

Sets the range and prices, and how many orders each slot can take from the pickers and vans on shift.

Picker

Walks the store with a pick list in aisle order, weighs, substitutes by the customer's rules, and packs by temperature.

Driver

Runs a van route of drops within the slot, settles substitutions at the door and checks ID for age-restricted items.

An order's life, against the slot

  1. MonSlot reserved. Thursday 18:00–19:00 has space; it's held for the customer for a couple of hours while they shop. customer
  2. MonBasket built. Each line gets a substitution rule; weighed items are priced at an estimate. customer
  3. MonChecked out. Over the minimum basket; the card is held for the estimate plus a margin; the slot is booked. customer
  4. Tue–WedAmended. Items added or removed, even the slot changed, until the cutoff the night before. customer
  5. Thu 10:00Pick list released. The slot's orders, merged and sorted into aisle order, split into ambient, chilled and frozen totes. picker
  6. ThuPicked. Weighed items priced at the scale; an empty shelf means a substitute by the customer's rule, or missing. picker
  7. Thu 17:15Loaded. Totes go on the van in reverse drop order, cold kept cold. driver
  8. Thu 17:30Told what changed. Substitutions and missing items, and a narrower arrival time, before the van arrives. platform
  9. Thu 18:20At the door. Substitutes accepted or handed back; ID checked for alcohol; signed for. driver
  10. Thu 19:00Charged. For what was delivered, at actual weights; the rest of the hold released; receipt sent. platform
  11. LaterPut right. Damaged, short-dated or missing items refunded. support

Words the domain uses

Slot
A delivery window, usually an hour, on a given day. Priced by demand: peak evenings cost more.
Slot reservation
A place in a slot held for one customer for a short time while they shop; it lapses unless they check out.
Capacity
How many orders a slot can take, set by vans, drops per route and pickers on shift.
Cutoff
The last moment an order can be amended, typically the night before the slot.
Amend
Changing a booked order (items, quantities, even the slot) before the cutoff.
Substitution rule
What the customer wants if an item is out: no substitute, anything similar, or a chosen alternative.
Substitute
The item picked in place of one that's out of stock; accepted or rejected at the door.
Pick list (pick walk)
A slot's orders merged into one route through the store, in aisle order.
Tote
The crate an order is packed into; one per temperature zone, often several per order.
Temperature zone
Ambient, chilled or frozen. Kept apart from pick to door.
Weighed item (catch weight)
Sold by weight, priced at an estimate until it's on the scale: meat, fish, loose fruit.
Drop
One delivery on a van route.
Minimum basket
The smallest order that can be checked out, or that avoids a small-basket charge.
Age-restricted item
Alcohol and the like: ID checked at the door, handed back if it fails.
Short-dated
Close to its use-by date. Grocers promise a minimum life on delivery.

Part 4

Open questions for you

  1. What does a time-slot reservation hold?A place in the slot's delivery capacity, stock for that day, or both? Grocers usually don't hold stock per item; the pick finds out what's there.
  2. How long is a slot held, and does an open hold count against capacity?Suggested: held for two hours, and yes, it counts, so a full slot is really full.
  3. Capacity: one number per slot, or worked out?Suggested: one number per slot in the skeleton; from vans, drops per route and pickers later.
  4. Substitution rules: per item, per order, or both?And who picks the substitute: the picker on the shop floor, a suggestion from the system, or the customer in advance?
  5. Payment: hold at checkout and charge after delivery?How big a margin over the estimate, and what happens when the final total is above it?
  6. Cutoff: how long before the slot, and what can change after it?Suggested: 22:00 the night before; nothing changes after it in the skeleton.
  7. One identity with roles, or separate accounts?Store operations, pickers and drivers need sign-in from Release 1.
  8. Geography in the sandbox.No map tiles or geocoding are reachable. One store, one delivery area of seeded postcodes, and drops ordered by a seeded sequence keep routes real enough.
